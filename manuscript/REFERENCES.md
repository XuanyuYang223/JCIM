# Primary references for the working manuscript

Metadata checked against the linked primary publication pages or authors' arXiv records on 1 November 2026. These references support the specific comparisons in the working draft. They do not establish novelty priority or validate the present experiments on chemical-property tasks.

1. **Gilmer, Justin; Schoenholz, Samuel S.; Riley, Patrick F.; Vinyals, Oriol; Dahl, George E.** Neural Message Passing for Quantum Chemistry. *Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning*, PMLR **70**, 1263–1272 (2017). [Publisher record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/gilmer17a.html). Used for the molecular message-passing framework, not as evidence for the present compositional results.

2. **Xu, Keyulu; Hu, Weihua; Leskovec, Jure; Jegelka, Stefanie.** How Powerful are Graph Neural Networks? *International Conference on Learning Representations* (2019). [Author preprint, arXiv:1810.00826](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826). Used for the neighborhood-aggregation perspective. The manuscript's attributed-neighborhood consistency statement is an architectural argument for the implemented model; it is not an assertion that every distinct motif must be linearly separable.

3. **Akhondzadeh, Mohammad Sadegh; Lingam, Vijay; Bojchevski, Aleksandar.** Probing Graph Representations. *Proceedings of the 26th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Statistics*, PMLR **206**, 11630–11649 (2023). [Publisher record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/akhondzadeh23a.html); [primary paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/akhondzadeh23a/akhondzadeh23a.pdf). Used for molecular representation probing and useful random-encoder features. The relevant random-versus-trained comparison appears in the paper's Table 5 and accompanying discussion.

4. **Kirichenko, Polina; Izmailov, Pavel; Wilson, Andrew Gordon.** Last Layer Re-Training is Sufficient for Robustness to Spurious Correlations. *International Conference on Learning Representations* (2023). [Author preprint, arXiv:2204.02937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02937); [primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.02937v2). Used for prior recovery through fixed-feature classifier refitting. Deep Feature Reweighting uses group-balanced reweighting data and specific regularization; the present source-only and 32-new-plus-32-old protocols are not claimed to reproduce that method.

5. **Kornblith, Simon; Norouzi, Mohammad; Lee, Honglak; Hinton, Geoffrey.** Similarity of Neural Network Representations Revisited. *Proceedings of the 36th International Conference on Machine Learning*, PMLR **97**, 3519–3529 (2019). [Publisher record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/kornblith19a.html). Used to identify the CKA comparison method. A geometric statistic is not used as a success criterion for compositional prediction in this draft.

6. **Ravichander, Abhilasha; Belinkov, Yonatan; Hovy, Eduard.** Probing the Probing Paradigm: Does Probing Accuracy Entail Task Relevance? *Proceedings of the 16th Conference of the European Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Main Volume*, 3363–3377 (2021). [Publisher record](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.295/). Used for the distinction between diagnostic decodability and task relevance. Its linguistic results motivate the distinction; they do not constitute molecular evidence.

## Relation to prior work

Neither random-feature readability nor recovery by fitting another classifier is claimed as a new general principle. The working contribution is the controlled molecular comparison of spatial pooling scope, label provenance, random encoders, labeled adaptation, and a boundary-free shallow-path intervention under explicit motif–position binding. The current experiments constrain where recoverability and prediction diverge, but do not establish a unique optimizer-level mechanism or priority over all existing molecular studies. A broader chemistry literature review remains necessary before submission.
